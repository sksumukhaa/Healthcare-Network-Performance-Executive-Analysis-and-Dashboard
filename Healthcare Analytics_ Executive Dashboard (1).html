Executive summary

Demand is balanced

All six conditions draw 9,095–9,218 patients each. No single condition needs priority capacity.

Costs are stable across patient groups

Average billing stays near $25.6K for every age group, with a spread of about 4%.

Payer mix is evenly split

Five insurers each cover 19.7%–20.3% of patients. No dependence on one payer.

One in three admissions is an emergency

18,102 emergency admissions, and a third of them return abnormal test results.

High-cost cases are a small tail

5,497 admissions (10%) sit in the top decile of billing and merit review.

Key business insights

Patients by medical condition

Arthritis leads with 9,218 patients, only 1.3% above Asthma at the low end.

Implication: staff specialists evenly. Past volumes do not favour any one condition.

Patients by age group

Ages 20–80 each hold about 8,000 patients. Under 20 and over 80 are the smallest groups.

Implication: the patient base is broad, with no age-driven demand spike.

Average billing by age group

Ages 10–20 are highest at $26,368; 80–90 lowest at $25,349.

Implication: age is not a meaningful driver of cost per admission.

Patients by insurance provider

Cigna covers the most patients (20.3%), just ahead of Medicare and UnitedHealthcare.

Implication: payer risk is diversified; negotiate with all five on equal footing.

Average billing per admission over time

Monthly averages stay within about $24.5K–$26.8K around the $25.6K network average, May 2019 – May 2024.

Implication: treatment cost per admission is flat, with no sustained rise or fall to plan around.

Additional insights

Emergency admissions by month

July is the busiest month (1,573); February the quietest (1,452), an 8% gap.

Implication: emergency staffing can stay near-constant year-round, with a modest buffer for mid-summer.

High-cost cases

5,497

Admissions above the 90th percentile of billing: 10.0% of all admissions.

Implication: a concentrated group worth reviewing for cost drivers and authorisation.

Emergencies with abnormal results

6,038

33.4% of emergency admissions, or 11.0% of all admissions.

Implication: a sizeable group needing follow-up care and higher-acuity resources.

Management takeaways

Balanced demand

What is happening
Patients spread evenly across conditions, ages and insurers.
Why it matters
No single segment drives volume or revenue risk.
Pay attention to
Keep capacity balanced; avoid over-investing in one specialty.

Flat cost profile

What is happening
Average billing is about $25.6K regardless of age or month.
Why it matters
Budgets and forecasts are predictable.
Pay attention to
The 5,497 high-cost cases, where cost control matters most.

Emergency load

What is happening
33% of admissions are emergencies; one in three has abnormal results.
Why it matters
These patients need the most urgent, resource-heavy care.
Pay attention to
Steady emergency staffing with a mid-summer buffer.
